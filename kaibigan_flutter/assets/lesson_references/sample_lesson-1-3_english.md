# Kaibigan — Friend Support Lessons & Quizzes

## Lesson 1 — Listen Before You Fix

### What You'll Learn

When a friend is struggling, your first job isn't always to solve their problem. Sometimes, what they need most is someone who will **listen without judging or immediately giving advice**.

You don't need to completely understand what they're going through. You can still show that you care.

### Helpful Responses

* “That sounds really difficult.”
* “I'm here if you want to talk.”
* “I might not fully understand, but I want to listen.”
* “You don't have to figure everything out right now.”

### Try to Avoid

* “Just don't think about it.”
* “Other people have it worse.”
* “Here's what you should do…”
* “Everything will be fine.”

---

### Question 1

**Your friend says:**

> “I've been feeling really overwhelmed lately. I don't know who I can talk to.”

**What would be the most supportive response?**

**A.** “Everyone feels overwhelmed sometimes. You just need to relax.”

**B.** “You should make a schedule so you can manage everything better.”

**C.** “I'm sorry you're going through that. I'm here to listen if you want to talk about what's been happening.”

**D.** “Don't worry, everything will work itself out.”

**💡 Help**

Think about what would make your friend feel **safe enough to open up**, rather than immediately trying to solve the problem.

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
It acknowledges that your friend is struggling and gives them space to talk. You aren't pretending to have all the answers—you're simply showing that you're present and willing to listen.

**Why A is less helpful:**
“Everyone feels overwhelmed” may be true, but it can make your friend's feelings seem ordinary or unimportant.

**Why B is less helpful:**
Practical advice isn't necessarily bad, but giving solutions before understanding the problem can make someone feel unheard.

**Why D is less helpful:**
Trying to reassure someone that “everything will work out” can unintentionally dismiss how serious or painful things feel to them right now.

---

### Question 2

**Your friend tells you about a painful experience, but you aren't sure what to say. What should you do?**

**A.** Change the subject so they don't feel uncomfortable.

**B.** Immediately tell them about a similar experience you've had.

**C.** Listen, acknowledge what they're saying, and ask gentle questions if they want to continue.

**D.** Give them several solutions for how they can fix the situation.

**💡 Help**

You don't need the perfect words. Focus on **being present and making space for them**.

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
Listening and gently acknowledging their experience shows that you care without taking control of the conversation.

**Why A is less helpful:**
Changing the subject may communicate that you're uncomfortable hearing about their feelings.

**Why B is less helpful:**
Sharing your own experience can sometimes help, but immediately making the conversation about yourself can take attention away from your friend.

**Why D is less helpful:**
A list of solutions can feel overwhelming, especially when the person may simply need someone to listen first.

---

### Question 3

**Your friend says:**

> “I feel like nobody understands me.”

**What is the best response?**

**A.** “I understand exactly how you feel.”

**B.** “You shouldn't feel that way. You have people who care about you.”

**C.** “I may not completely understand what you're going through, but I care about you and I'm willing to listen.”

**D.** “You just need to think more positively.”

**💡 Help**

You don't have to claim that you understand everything. **Honest empathy is better than pretending.**

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
It recognizes that you may not have the same experience while still communicating genuine care and willingness to listen.

**Why A is less helpful:**
Saying you understand exactly how someone feels can unintentionally minimize their unique experience.

**Why B is less helpful:**
It argues against their feelings instead of first acknowledging them.

**Why D is less helpful:**
“Think positively” oversimplifies emotional struggles and can make someone feel blamed for how they feel.

# Lesson 2 — Take Suicidal Thoughts Seriously

### What You'll Learn

When someone says they are thinking about suicide or hurting themselves, **take them seriously**.

You don't need to panic or have the perfect response. Stay calm, listen, show care, and help connect them with appropriate support.

You can also ask directly if they're thinking about hurting themselves. Asking about suicide **doesn't mean you're putting the idea into their head**.

### Remember

* Listen without judging.
* Take suicidal statements seriously.
* Ask directly when you're concerned about their safety.
* Don't guilt-trip them.
* Don't promise to keep dangerous situations secret.
* Get additional help when someone's safety may be at risk.

---

### Question 1

**Your friend says:**

> “I've been thinking about killing myself. I don't know how much longer I can do this.”

**What is the most supportive response?**

**A.** “Think about your family. They would be devastated if you did that.”

**B.** “You should pray more and go to church.”

**C.** “I might not fully understand what you're going through, but I'm here to listen and help you through this. I don't want you to face this alone.”

**D.** “Don't say that. You have so much to live for.”

**💡 Help**

Focus on **acknowledging their pain and staying connected**, rather than trying to guilt, debate, or immediately dismiss what they said.

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
It responds with compassion, avoids judgment, and communicates that the person doesn't have to face the situation alone. In a real crisis, this should be followed by getting appropriate help and making sure they're not left alone when immediate danger is present.

**Why A is harmful:**
Using family members to create guilt can increase shame and emotional pressure rather than helping the person feel supported.

**Why B is not enough:**
Faith can be meaningful to someone, but telling them to simply pray or attend church does not address an immediate safety concern and should never replace appropriate crisis support.

**Why D is less helpful:**
It may be intended as encouragement, but it argues against the person's feelings instead of first taking their pain seriously.

---

### Question 2

**Your friend says:**

> “Sometimes I wish I could just disappear.”

**What is the best response if you're concerned about their safety?**

**A.** “Don't talk like that. You're just having a bad day.”

**B.** “What do you mean? Are you thinking about hurting yourself?”

**C.** “You shouldn't say things like that because people might misunderstand you.”

**D.** “Let's not talk about negative things right now.”

**💡 Help**

When you're genuinely concerned about someone's safety, **don't avoid the question just because it feels uncomfortable**.

**✅ Correct Answer: B**

**Why B is right:**
It directly checks whether your friend may be thinking about self-harm or suicide. Asking calmly and without judgment can help you understand how serious the situation is and what support is needed.

**Why A is harmful:**
It dismisses what your friend said and assumes you already know what they're experiencing.

**Why C is harmful:**
It focuses on how their words might be perceived rather than on their wellbeing.

**Why D is harmful:**
Changing the subject can leave an important safety concern unaddressed.

---

### Question 3

**Your friend tells you they are thinking about suicide and asks you to promise that you won't tell anyone. What should you do?**

**A.** “I promise. You can trust me.”

**B.** “Fine, I'll tell everyone so they can watch you.”

**C.** “I care about your trust, but if I think you're in danger, I may need to involve someone who can help keep you safe.”

**D.** “Okay, then I won't talk to you about it anymore.”

**💡 Help**

Think about the difference between **respecting someone's privacy and keeping a dangerous secret**.

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
It respects the person's trust while making it clear that safety comes first when there may be immediate danger. In a serious situation, involving a trusted adult, family member, professional, crisis service, or emergency service may be necessary.

**Why A is harmful:**
A promise of secrecy can prevent the person from receiving the help they need.

**Why B is harmful:**
Telling everyone unnecessarily can violate their privacy and potentially make them feel exposed or betrayed.

**Why D is harmful:**
Ending the conversation can make the person feel abandoned at a moment when connection may be especially important.

# Lesson 3 — You Don't Have to Handle It Alone

### What You'll Learn

Being a supportive friend doesn't mean becoming someone's therapist or taking complete responsibility for their wellbeing.

You can **listen, stay present, encourage professional help, involve trusted people, and ask for help yourself**.

Supporting someone doesn't mean carrying everything alone.

### Helpful Actions

* Listen and check in.
* Encourage them to seek professional support.
* Help them contact someone they trust.
* Stay with them when appropriate during a crisis.
* Ask for help when the situation is beyond what you can safely handle.

### Avoid

* “I'm the only person you need.”
* “Don't tell anyone else.”
* Taking complete responsibility for their recovery.
* Abandoning them with “Just see a therapist.”

---

### Question 1

**Your friend has been struggling for weeks. They've stopped attending school, isolated themselves, and say:**

> “I don't think I can handle this anymore.”

**What is the best response?**

**A.** “You need professional help. Just make an appointment.”

**B.** “Don't worry, I'll handle this. You don't need to tell anyone else.”

**C.** “I'm really glad you told me. I care about you, and I think it would be good to get some support from someone who can help. I can stay with you while we figure out who to reach out to.”

**D.** “Maybe you just need to take a break from everything for a few days.”

**💡 Help**

Look for an answer that combines **compassion + practical support**, without pretending that you alone can fix everything.

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
It shows care, encourages additional support, and offers to help the person take the next step instead of leaving them to figure everything out alone.

**Why A is less helpful:**
Professional help may be appropriate, but simply telling someone to “make an appointment” can feel cold or dismissive.

**Why B is harmful:**
One friend should not become someone's only source of support, especially when serious safety concerns may exist.

**Why D is less helpful:**
Rest can sometimes help with stress, but this response may underestimate the seriousness of the situation.

---

### Question 2

**Your friend says:**

> “I don't want to talk to anyone else. You're the only person I trust.”

**What is the healthiest response?**

**A.** “That's okay. I'll be the only person you need.”

**B.** “Then you need to figure this out yourself.”

**C.** “I'm glad you trust me. I'll listen and support you, but I also want to help you find other people who can support you too.”

**D.** “You shouldn't depend on friends for emotional problems.”

**💡 Help**

A good friend can provide support without becoming the person's **entire support system**.

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
It values the trust between you while encouraging a wider support network. Different people can provide different kinds of help.

**Why A is harmful:**
It creates unhealthy dependence and puts an unrealistic amount of responsibility on one person.

**Why B is harmful:**
It abandons the person when they're already struggling.

**Why D is less helpful:**
Friends can absolutely provide emotional support. The goal isn't to remove friendship from the equation—it's to make sure the person has enough support.

---

### Question 3

**You are supporting a friend who is struggling, but you're starting to feel overwhelmed yourself. What should you do?**

**A.** Keep everything to yourself because their problems are more important.

**B.** Tell your friend that they're becoming a burden.

**C.** Set healthy boundaries and reach out to someone you trust for support while continuing to care about your friend.

**D.** Stop responding to your friend completely.

**💡 Help**

Remember: **supporting someone doesn't require sacrificing your own wellbeing or handling everything alone.**

**✅ Correct Answer: C**

**Why C is right:**
You can care deeply about someone while recognizing your own limits. Getting support for yourself can help you remain a healthier and more reliable friend.

**Why A is harmful:**
Ignoring your own limits can lead to exhaustion and make it harder for you to support anyone effectively.

**Why B is harmful:**
Calling someone a burden can increase shame and make them less likely to seek help.

**Why D is harmful:**
Suddenly disappearing can make the person feel abandoned. If you need space, communicate your boundaries rather than disappearing without explanation.

Every lesson involving serious emotional distress or self-harm should include an easily accessible **“Need Help?”** button.

The button should provide appropriate crisis, emergency, and professional-support resources based on the user's location.

The feature should also remind users:

> **You don't have to handle a crisis alone. If someone may be in immediate danger, involve a trusted person or appropriate emergency/crisis support.**

The goal of these lessons is **not to turn users into therapists**. It is to teach them how to respond with empathy, recognize potentially dangerous situations, and know when to involve additional help.
